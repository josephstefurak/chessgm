# Decisions

Judgment calls made while building, newest first. Entries are updated when revisited, never deleted.

| Date | Decision | Where | Why | Client confirmation needed | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-11-13 | Browser GUI security: binds to 127.0.0.1, Host allow-list, exact-Origin check on every POST (missing Origin rejected), 1 KB body cap, strict schemas, CSP with no inline script. Two omissions on purpose: no HSTS, no per-launch token | `webgui/server/security.py` | A page open in the browser can reach a local server, so Host and Origin checks are needed even with no accounts. HSTS does not apply to plain HTTP on loopback. A token would stop other programs on this Mac from posting moves, but the only thing at stake is a chess game and it would add a login step | No | Confirmed |
| 2026-11-13 | The board is chessground, so `webgui/` is offered under GPL-3.0 (its own `LICENSE` file) | `webgui/` | Chosen by the owner for the best drag and animation feel. chessground is GPL-3.0-or-later; python-chess, already a dependency, is GPL-3.0 too. react-chessboard (MIT) and a custom board were the alternatives | No | Confirmed |
| 2026-11-13 | Sounds are synthesized in the browser with WebAudio; no audio files | `webgui/web/src/audio/sounds.ts` | Recorded samples carry their own licences. Chosen by the owner. Recorded files can replace the synth later without touching anything else | No | Confirmed |
| 2026-11-13 | `gui.py` refuses to start, with the exact build command, if the UI has not been built; it never runs `npm` itself | `gui.py` | A launcher that silently runs a package manager is surprising and slow to fail. Auto-building on first run was the alternative | No | Confirmed |
| 2026-11-13 | No premoves, no difficulty levels, no analysis while it is the human's turn, nothing saved between launches | `webgui/` | The owner asked for something simple at full strength. Premoves complicate a server-owned game; analysis on the human's turn would need a second search that competes with nothing useful. The eval bar shows the engine's last search instead | Yes: are premoves wanted later? | Pending |
| 2026-11-13 | Engine insight panel is visible by default and can be hidden; the choice is remembered in the browser | `webgui/web/src/components/EnginePanel.tsx` | The owner asked for the panel, so hiding it by default would bury it. Hidden-by-default (to avoid spoiling the game) was the alternative | Yes: visible or hidden by default? | Pending |
| 2026-11-13 | GUI uses the opening book in timed and untimed games | `webgui/server/engine_port.py` | `main.py` always uses the book; `uci.py` only on clocked moves. The GUI is local play, so it follows `main.py` | No | Confirmed |
| 2026-11-13 | GUI game end: threefold repetition and the 50-move rule end the game automatically, but only once the position has actually occurred three times (or 100 half-moves have passed); running out of time when the opponent cannot possibly mate is a draw | `webgui/server/session.py` `_ending` | Revised while building. The first version reused `main.py`'s `outcome(claim_draw=True)`, which also ends the game when the side to move merely could repeat on its next move. A test showed the draw being called one move before the board showed it, which in a GUI also stops a winning player from choosing to play on. `main.py` is unchanged. The timeout rule is the FIDE one and what Lichess does | No | Revised |
| 2026-11-13 | GUI takeback is allowed in timed games and restores both clocks; it takes back one ply while the engine is thinking and two otherwise; it is not offered once the game is over | `webgui/server/session.py` | Against a bot there is no opponent to refuse, and a takeback that kept the spent time would be a penalty nobody asked for. After the game, "New game" is the clearer action | No | Confirmed |
| 2026-11-13 | GUI time controls are five presets: Untimed, 3+2, 5+3, 10+0, 15+10. Each side's clock starts after its own first move | `webgui/server/app.py`, `webgui/server/clock.py` | Common blitz and rapid controls; a custom control was left out to keep the dialog simple. Starting the clock after the first move means nobody loses time while looking at a fresh board | Yes: are these the presets you want? | Pending |
| 2026-11-13 | GUI engine runs in-process on one long-lived worker thread, not as a UCI subprocess, and searches are cancelled by a generation number checked before the search and at every completed depth | `webgui/server/engine_port.py` | Measured: in-process costs the search about 3% speed and about 13 ms of request latency. `Searcher.search` clears its stop flag on entry, so a stop sent just before a search starts is lost; the generation check closes that gap without changing `engine/` | No | Confirmed |
| 2026-11-13 | In the GUI the server owns the game and the browser holds no chess rules: every snapshot carries legal destinations, promotions, SAN, a FEN per ply, clocks and result | `webgui/server/session.py`, `webgui/web/` | Keeps behaviour in Python where it is tested and keeps the browser as presentation only. A rules library in the browser (chess.js) was the alternative and would have put the same logic in two places | No | Confirmed |
| 2026-11-13 | Mobility term (squares reachable by knights, bishops, rooks, queens) was implemented, tested and removed | `engine/evaluation.py` | It made the eval 50% slower (12.4 to 18.5 microseconds) and scored 46.9% over 80 games against the same eval without it (95% CI 37.7% to 56.1%). Not at or above 50%, so dropped under the A/B rule. Worth retrying if the engine gets faster or the weights are tuned | No | Confirmed |
| 2026-11-13 | New evaluation (tapered, PeSTO tables, bishop pair, pawn shelter, endgame knowledge) kept | `engine/evaluation.py`, `engine/weights.py` | Scored 75.6% over 80 games at 0.25s/move against the first version of the new engine, which used the legacy eval terms with corrected signs (95% CI 67.3% to 83.9%, about +200 Elo). The candidate also had late move pruning on, which measured neutral on its own | No | Confirmed |
| 2026-11-13 | Pruning techniques all kept after A/B (on vs off, same engine): LMR 55.0% (80 games, 0.25s), reverse futility 56.2% (80, 0.25s), late move pruning 51.6% (64, 0.2s), null move 51.0% (48, 1.0s) and 48.8% (80, 0.25s), futility 50.8% (64, 0.2s), bad-capture pruning 50.0% (64, 0.2s), check extension 50.0% (64, 0.2s). Aspiration windows were not match-tested (2% fewer nodes at fixed depth) | `engine/search.py` | None is clearly negative; the confidence intervals are about plus or minus 10 points, so only LMR and reverse futility lean positive and the rest are statistically neutral. They are kept because each reduces the node count at fixed depth (LMR 2.7x, null move 1.24x, LMP 1.3x) and these techniques gain more as search depth grows | No | Confirmed |
| 2026-11-13 | Tried and dropped: searching the hash move before generating other moves; internal reductions when there is no hash move; a history penalty for quiet moves that failed to cut off | `engine/search.py` | Each was measured at fixed depth. Hash-move staging: same nodes per second. Internal reductions: 5% more nodes at a shallower effective depth. History penalty: 5 to 10% more nodes. None is in the code | No | Confirmed |
| 2026-11-13 | Late move pruning thresholds: at depth 1/2/3 only the first 7/16/31 quiet moves are searched | `engine/search.py` | Standard technique; the formula `4 + 3 * depth^2` is a common starting point, not tuned. Measured 23% fewer nodes at depth 7 and 51.6% in a 64-game A/B (neutral within error, kept under the A/B rule) | No | Confirmed |
| 2026-11-13 | With no pawns and less than 400cp of extra piece material, the leading side's score is cut to a quarter | `engine/evaluation.py`, `PAWNLESS_WIN_MARGIN` | Rook vs bishop, rook and bishop vs rook and similar endings are normally drawn; without this the engine trades into them thinking it is winning. 400 separates those from queen vs rook and rook vs nothing, which are wins | No | Confirmed |
| 2026-11-13 | "Unstoppable pawn" bonus now requires a passed pawn with an empty path, and counts once per side | `engine/evaluation.py` | The legacy rule gave 400cp for every pawn the king could not reach, including blocked pawns and pawns with enemy pawns in front, and stacked for several pawns | No | Confirmed |
| 2026-11-13 | Eval weights other than the piece-square tables (passed pawns, doubled/isolated, rook files, bishop pair, pawn shield, tempo, endgame bonuses) are hand-picked starting values | `engine/weights.py` | There is no tuning data yet; learning is deferred. Values are conventional magnitudes. They are the obvious first target for regression tuning | No | Confirmed |
| 2026-11-13 | Piece values and piece-square tables are the published PeSTO set, used unchanged | `engine/weights.py` | They are regression-tuned values that are widely used as a baseline, and they come as middlegame/endgame pairs, which the tapered eval needs. The legacy hand-written tables had no endgame versions except for the king. Source and author are credited in the file | No | Confirmed |
| 2026-11-13 | Local play thinks for at most `--time` seconds (default 2) and stops starting new depths at half of that | `main.py` | The old fixed depth 3 took 9 to 36s per move. 2s was chosen as a wait that feels responsive; the half-way rule means the usual wait is nearer 1s. Fixed depth was rejected because its cost varies 100x between positions | Yes: is 2s the feel you want? | Pending |
| 2026-11-13 | A `go` command that cannot be parsed thinks for 1 second and still answers; a bare `go` with no limits searches until `stop` | `uci.py` | A GUI that sent a broken `go` is waiting for a `bestmove`, so refusing would hang the game. Bare `go` meaning "infinite" is what the UCI spec says; the legacy wrapper used a 2s default instead | No | Confirmed |
| 2026-11-13 | Time per move: remaining clock / 40 plus 80% of the increment; stop starting new depths at 55% of that; hard stop at 2.5x, never above half the remaining clock | `engine/timeman.py` | Legacy used clock / 30 capped at 10s, ignored increments, and always burned the full slice. 40 moves is a common planning horizon; the soft/hard split exists because an unfinished depth is mostly wasted. A 10+0 game opens at about 8s per move | No | Confirmed |
| 2026-11-13 | Depth 1 always runs to completion, even past the time limit | `engine/search.py` | Guarantees the move played has been searched. Legacy returned the first legal move in the list when nothing finished. Cost: up to about 50ms over budget in the worst position measured | No | Confirmed |
| 2026-11-13 | Each pruning technique is switchable through a UCI option (NullMove, LMR, ...) | `uci.py`, `engine/search.py` `SearchConfig` | Lets `tools/match.py` A/B test one technique at a time without code edits. Exposing tunables as UCI options is the standard way engines are tested. Environment variables were considered and rejected as hidden configuration | No | Confirmed |
| 2026-11-13 | Transposition table is a Python dict keyed by `hash()` of the position key, cleared when full; `Hash` option maps MB to entries at roughly 2,500 per MB | `engine/search.py`, `uci.py` | Keeping the full key tuple per entry costs about 500 bytes; the 61-bit hash is enough and a wrong hash move is harmless because moves are only ordered, never played, from the table. The per-MB figure is an estimate, not measured | No | Confirmed |
| 2026-11-13 | A/B rule for strength changes: play the candidate against the previous version at a fast time control; keep at 50% or better, drop if clearly negative | `tools/match.py`, every search and eval change | Search and eval ideas that are "standard" still lose strength when implemented badly or when the engine is too slow to benefit; measuring is the only way to know | No | Confirmed (in approved plan) |
| 2026-11-13 | `chess` pinned to 1.11.2 and `Board._transposition_key()` kept as the position key | `requirements.txt`, `engine/search.py` | It is a private API, but the public `chess.polyglot.zobrist_hash` recomputes the hash from scratch on every call and is far slower. Pinning makes the dependency on the private method safe | No | Confirmed (in approved plan) |
| 2026-11-13 | Draws score 0 with no contempt. Repetition, 50-move and insufficient material are all scored as draws inside the search | `engine/search.py` | Replaces the legacy "300cp penalty for repeating when ahead on material" and the stalemate skip. A winning side avoids a 0 score on its own; a losing side now correctly seeks it. Contempt was considered and left out because the bot's eval is not reliable enough to know when it is truly better | No | Confirmed (in approved plan) |
| 2026-11-13 | Legacy engine (`bot.py`, `evaluator.py`, `features.py`, `neural_net.py`, `train.py`, `train_neural.py`) is frozen, not patched or deleted. The new engine lives in `engine/` | whole repo | Working agreement says build forward. The legacy files are still what the training scripts use, and they serve as the baseline opponent for A/B matches. Their known bugs are fixed by moving the playing path off them | No | Confirmed (in approved plan) |
| 2026-11-13 | The neural net is not used for play | `uci.py`, `main.py` | Measured: its output is noise (says White is +20cp with White missing a queen; swings a median of 168cp across quiet moves in one equal position). Learning is deferred | No | Confirmed (in approved plan) |
